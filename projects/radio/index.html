    transmission notes

    This is not a playlist. It is a broadcast. There is no pause button and no skip. The station has been playing since its epoch and the page simply computes where the signal is right now — so every listener, on every continent, hears the same track at the same second. You don't press play; you tune in.

    Freq A rotates roughly five hours of jungle, ragga jungle, dub, and drum & bass from the netlabel underground — artist-released, Creative Commons–licensed records from labels that gave their music away on purpose. Every track credits its source and links back to the original release.

    Freq B is the video-game signal: jungle and breakbeat written as tracker modules — the .mod and .xm files of the Amiga and demoscene era that PlayStation-generation soundtracks grew out of. The modules stream from The Mod Archive and are synthesized live in your browser; the readout under the spectrum shows the pattern and row actually being executed. A new module takes the slot every half hour, on the half hour, for everyone at once.

    The receiver panel above is the honest part: a live wire into what I'm personally listening to, scrobbled from Spotify. The broadcast is what I can legally play you; the receiver is what I'd play you if the record industry were a different thing.

    No backend, no stream server, no framework. One HTML file, a fixed epoch, and arithmetic.
