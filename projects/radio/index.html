    transmission notes

    This is not a playlist. It is a broadcast. There is no pause and no skip. The station has been playing since its epoch and the page simply computes where the signal is right now — so every listener, on every continent, hears the same track at the same second. You don't press play; you tune in.

    The crate is eleven hours of jungle, ragga jungle, dub, and drum & bass from twenty-one releases across the netlabel underground — artist-released, Creative Commons–licensed records from labels that gave their music away on purpose. And it never plays the same show twice: every cycle the rotation reshuffles itself deterministically, so the order is always new but every listener still computes the identical sequence — the playlist shows the actual clock time each record will air where you are. Woven between them are tracker interludes: jungle written as .mod and .xm modules, the file format PlayStation-era game soundtracks grew out of, streamed from The Mod Archive and synthesized live in your browser while the status bar shows the pattern and row being executed.

    The player is an ode to the media players this music was first heard through — and the visualization behind it is the part of Windows Media Player everyone actually remembers. It flies through a node field that steers, accelerates, and shifts color with the frequencies of whatever the station is playing. Every track credits its source and links back to the original release.

    The receiver panel is the honest part: a live wire into what I'm personally listening to, scrobbled from Spotify. The broadcast is what I can legally play you; the receiver is what I'd play you if the record industry were a different thing.

    No backend, no stream server, no framework. One HTML file, a fixed epoch, and arithmetic.
