Field Notes

what the light actually says

A raw complaint map mostly shows where people live and who calls 311. These notes control for that — each one links to a configured view of the map.

01

The Bronx runs hot — and cold

Heat, hot-water, and plumbing complaints are 34% over-represented in the Bronx relative to its share of all 311 calls — the strongest borough signature of any layer. In signature mode the borough burns ember: this isn't where the most people call, it's where the calls are disproportionately about the building itself failing.

02

Trash waits longest in the Bronx

Sanitation complaints get closed in a median of 6 days in the Bronx versus 2 in Queens — only 12% are resolved same-day there, half the rate of Queens. Of the five layers, sanitation shows the widest gap between boroughs in how long the city takes to show up. The full breakdown is in the response-time table below.

03

Manhattan is the rat capital — per capita of complaint

Brooklyn files the most rodent reports in raw numbers, but relative to each borough's overall 311 volume, Manhattan's rodent share runs 44% above the citywide rate. On the map, the rodent layer traces the pre-war brownstone belt — Bed-Stuy, Harlem, the Upper West Side — with almost surgical precision.

04

Noise is nocturnal, parking is a morning ritual

Noise complaints peak at midnight, led by “loud music/party”; parking complaints crest with the morning commute. Scrub the rhythm slider from 6am to 2am and the city visibly changes shifts — gold fades out as violet blooms. Worth knowing: over 90% of noise complaints are closed the same day, which says more about how the NYPD clears tickets than how fast the party stopped.

05

Queens would like a word about your car

Illegal parking, blocked driveways, and abandoned vehicles are 45% over-represented in Queens — the most car-dependent borough with the most driveways to block. It's the single largest complaint group in the city: nearly 900,000 calls in twelve months, and effectively all of them closed within a day.

Response Times

how long until the city shows up

Share of complaints closed the same day they were filed, by borough — with the median days to close. Closure means the agency marked it resolved, which is not always the same as fixed.

Method

how this was built

Roughly 3.3 million service requests from NYC Open Data's 311 dataset (July 2025 – July 2026), aggregated server-side with SoQL into a ~110-meter grid — the browser never sees a raw row. Five complaint families, log-scaled and rendered additively on canvas. No map tiles: the street grid you see is the complaints themselves.

signature mode

Each cell is colored by its most over-represented complaint family — the layer whose local share most exceeds its citywide share (a location quotient). Population density cancels out of the ratio, so the map shows character, not crowding.

freshness

The dataset refreshes daily. The “last 24h” layer and headline counts are fetched live from the API on page load; the twelve-month baseline is a static extract, rebuilt on a schedule.

caveats

311 measures the propensity to complain, not the underlying condition — a quiet block may simply have given up. Neighborhood labels are nearest-centroid approximations. Same-day closure is an administrative act, and for some agencies a generous one.
