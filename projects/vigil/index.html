About This Instrument

a map of the world's open wounds

Most of what happens to the planet never reaches a feed you follow. This page holds vigil: every earthquake, wildfire, flood, storm, outbreak, and eruption currently registered by the systems that watch for them — alongside the slower catastrophes that no sensor detects.

detected

The moving lights are machine-witnessed. Earthquakes arrive from the USGS within minutes of the ground settling. NASA's EONET tracks active wildfires and eruptions by satellite. GDACS — the UN's disaster alert system — scores floods, cyclones, and droughts by severity. The WHO publishes outbreak bulletins: as of this snapshot, that includes an active Ebola outbreak in the DRC and Uganda, Nipah virus in India, and a hantavirus cluster spreading through cruise ships. These layers refresh themselves; nobody chooses what appears.

witnessed

The steady embers are different. No API fires an event when a famine begins, and nothing alerts when the world stops paying attention. Each ongoing crisis on this map — Gaza, Sudan, eastern Congo, Myanmar, Haiti, the Sahel, Ukraine, Yemen, Afghanistan — is curated by hand, with figures drawn from UN OCHA, the IPC, UNHCR, and conflict-data projects, and a day counter that does not stop. The crises people know least about are not the ones lacking sensors. They are the ones lacking witnesses.

the night line

The shadow crossing the map is the actual terminator — the line between day and night on Earth at this moment, computed from the clock. It is there as a reminder that this is not an archive. All of it is happening now.

method

No map tiles and no libraries: coastlines and borders are Natural Earth geometry rendered to canvas, events are drawn as light, and the page is a single HTML file. Feeds are fetched live on load; the curated register is a JSON file with cited sources, reviewed by hand.

how to help

Every ongoing crisis on the map lists organizations working inside it — click an ember and the card carries direct links. If choosing feels paralyzing, the honest default is an unrestricted gift to a field organization with global reach: Médecins Sans Frontières, the World Food Programme, the ICRC, or the IRC. Unrestricted means they can send it where the map is darkest, not where the headlines are.

caveats

Feed coverage is not suffering coverage — a magnitude 4.5 near a city matters more than a 6.0 under the ocean, and no severity score captures a school year lost or a hospital closed. Day counts mark duration, not importance. The register reflects editorial judgment and its figures should be read with their dates.

Known blind spots: heat waves and cold snaps (no public feed tracks them well, though they kill more people than most of what is shown), day-to-day conflict violence (event data lags weeks and needs licensing; the register carries the standing crises instead), and small earthquakes outside the US, where the global seismic network's floor is roughly magnitude 4. Every feed sees the world unevenly — finding the gaps was half the work.
